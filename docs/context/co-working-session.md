# The co-working session

**Working note.** A picture of how a person and an agent work together, used as a loose check on
what this repo generates. Not a skill and not a formal eval. Hampton proposed the framing on
2026-10-13 ("two people are sitting down for a co-working session"); Claude wrote the description
below and Hampton confirmed it as "a good representation of the flow". It came out of the session
recorded in [open-questions.md](open-questions.md), "When does talk become a request to build?".

## The picture

Two people at a table. One of them owns the project.

- **Most of the time is talk, and it wanders.** Ideas come up, get turned over, and many are
  dropped. Nobody treats each one as a work order.
- **Ideas get parked, not started.** Someone writes the idea on the whiteboard and the conversation
  moves on. The note is the whole action.
- **Small things happen without asking.** Looking up a fact, pulling up a document or sketching a
  diagram serves the conversation and costs nothing to undo.
- **Bigger things are announced or offered.** "Want me to mock that up?" comes at a natural pause,
  and only now and then. Each can see the other reach for the keyboard, so nothing starts unseen.
- **The owner calls the shift.** At some point one of them says "OK, what are we actually doing?"
  That is the turn from wandering to a spec, and on the owner's project it is the owner's to call.
- **A good partner reads the phase.** While ideas are still opening up they add to them, and they
  save the narrowing for when the talk converges.

Hampton singled out two of these as the key points (2026-10-13):

- **Eventually work has to get done.** In his words, "someone is likely to call that out and have
  the group focus on a task". Wandering is not the end state; the session converges when someone
  names the task. Open: "someone" is wider than the bullet above, which gives the call to the owner.
  Whether the agent may be the one to call it out, and how often, is not settled.
- **The good partner reads the phase.** Knowing whether the talk is opening up or closing in is
  what tells a partner whether to add, to park, or to call for focus.

A third point from Hampton, the same day:

- **Good sessions produce things beside the task.** One person recommends an article or a book, or
  offers to connect the other with someone who can help. In his words, "focusing too narrowly
  prevents good work from being done". Claude's reading of the agent's version: a reference, a piece
  of prior art, a person or community worth asking, or a link to the owner's other work, offered in
  a line and not pursued unless taken up. It pulls against `simplicity` rule 8 (volume is a cost),
  so the offer has to stay small. Hampton on who pays for that: one line keeps the listener's cost
  low, and "the cost is being paid by the person taking a moment to figure out what's happening and
  say the right message in a few words". The same sentiment in Pascal, near the end of the
  sixteenth of his *Lettres provinciales* (collected 1657): "Je n'ai fait celle-ci plus longue que
  parce que je n'ai pas eu le loisir de la faire plus courte", in a modern translation "I have made
  this one longer only because I did not have the leisure to make it shorter."[^pascal]

Hampton's words for the wandering part: the agent is used to research and understand the lay of the
land as well as to build, "like jotting down notes as i'm browsing"; the notes may go through a spec
pass later; "it is ok for creativity to wander".

## Three kinds of action

| Kind | Examples | When |
|---|---|---|
| Done freely | look up, read, measure | whenever it serves the conversation |
| Parked | an idea, a possible build, a follow-up | written down, then the talk moves on |
| Waits for the shift | building, changing, sending | after the owner calls it |

Where the line falls between "done freely" and "waits" is not settled.

## Where the picture breaks with an agent

| Between people | With an agent |
|---|---|
| Starting work takes effort, which is a natural brake | Acting is nearly free, so the brake has to be a rule |
| Each can see the other start | The agent has to say what it is about to do |
| Both remember the whiteboard tomorrow | Parked ideas must be written somewhere durable |

## Using it as a loose check

Hold a candidate rule, a skill change, or a session transcript against the picture and ask:

- Would it have the partner interrupt a wandering conversation to ask for a decision?
- Would it stop a small lookup that serves the conversation?
- Does anything start that the owner could not see starting?
- Does a parked idea end up somewhere that outlives the session?
- Who calls the shift from talk to spec, and from spec to build?
- Does it steer toward a deliverable while the talk is still opening up?
- Does it leave room for a reference or a connection that is beside the task?

A "yes" to the first, second, third or sixth, a "no" to the last, or a parked idea with no home, is
a reason to look again. It is a judgement aid; nothing here is measured, and `evals/` holds no case
for it.

## Two failures it was drawn from

Both from the 2026-10-13 session. The agent started building on a stated preference (an act that
should have waited for the shift). It also kept steering the conversation back to the next step of
a task ("have you filed the feedback form yet?") while the owner was still exploring.
